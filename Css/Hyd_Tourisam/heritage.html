A City Built on Royal Legacy

Founded by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah in 1591, Hyderabad is home to some of India's most magnificent Islamic and Indo-Persian monuments. These heritage sites tell the story of empires, art and architectural genius that shaped an entire civilisation.

★★★★★

Charminar

Built in 1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah to commemorate the founding of Hyderabad and to pray for an end to a deadly plague, Charminar is the iconic symbol of the city. The name literally means "Four Towers." The structure stands 56 metres tall with four ornate minarets. Its upper floor houses a mosque, and the archways open into the vibrant Laad Bazaar nearby.

Golconda Fort

Golconda Fort dates from the 13th century and became the seat of the Qutb Shahi dynasty in the 16th century. It was renowned as the world's most important diamond trading centre — the mighty Koh-i-Noor and Hope Diamond once passed through here. Today its acoustic engineering, where a hand clap at the entrance can be heard at the summit, still astonishes visitors. The evening Sound & Light Show is a must-see experience.

★★★★★

Chowmahalla Palace

Chowmahalla Palace was the official residence of the Nizams of Hyderabad. Built in the 18th–19th centuries, it was modelled after the Shah's palace in Tehran. The palace complex features two courtyards — the Outer Courtyard (Bāhar i Chowmahalla) and the Inner Courtyard (Andar i Chowmahalla) — with ornate Khilwat Mubarak durbar hall, vintage car collection and stunning European-inspired architecture.

Qutb Shahi Tombs

A necropolis of seven magnificent granite tombs housing the remains of the Qutb Shahi rulers and their families. Built between the 16th and 17th centuries, each tomb is set on a raised platform within a garden. The architectural style reflects a unique blend of Persian, Pashtun and Hindu traditions. This site is nominated for UNESCO World Heritage Site status.

Mecca Masjid

One of the largest mosques in India and among the oldest in Hyderabad, Mecca Masjid was built between 1614 and 1694. Its name comes from the bricks made from soil brought from Mecca, used in the central arch. The mosque can accommodate over 10,000 worshippers at a time. The tombs of the Nizams of Hyderabad are located in a small enclosure to the left of the main entrance.

Paigah Tombs

The Paigah Tombs are the burial grounds of the noble Paigah family, close allies of the Nizam dynasty. Built in the 18th–19th centuries, these tombs feature exquisite stucco plasterwork, Italian marble inlays and intricate jali (lattice) work. Often called the "Forgotten Jewels of Hyderabad," they represent some of the finest funerary architecture in India.

Colonial Heritage  
★★★★☆

British Residency (Koti)

Built in 1803 by James Achilles Kirkpatrick, the British Resident at the Nizam's court, this magnificent colonial mansion is a breathtaking Indo-Saracenic structure. It was famously the subject of William Dalrymple's acclaimed book "White Mughals." Today the building serves as the Koti Women's College. The grand entrance hall and Durbar Hall are architectural masterpieces.

Cultural Complex  
★★★★☆

Taramati Baradari

Taramati Baradari (also known as Premamati Baradari) is a 17th-century sarai and entertainment complex built by Abdullah Qutb Shah for the court dancer Taramati. It features 12 arches (Baradari means "twelve doors") and a unique open amphitheatre. Today it hosts cultural events, music festivals, and the annual Taramati music festival. The view of Golconda Fort from here is spectacular.
